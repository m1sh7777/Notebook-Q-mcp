        How NotebookQ works
        
      

      

        
Draw a circuit: a long flat line becomes a qubit wire.
        Box a letter on it for a gate — H (split), X (flip), Z (sign flip),
        R (rotation by a variable angle). The odds of every bit pattern
        appear in the panel below.

        
CNOT: draw a near-vertical line between two wires, with a
        small circle-blob on the TARGET wire (a dot on the control wire is
        optional). H, then a CNOT, makes the Bell state — two entangled
        qubits.

        
Teach: click a letter in the toolbar, draw that letter a
        few times (pause after each), click again to finish. Your examples
        make recognition match YOUR handwriting.

        
Variables: type a name and a number (theta = 1.57), press
        Add. When you place an R box, a dropdown asks which variable to use.
        Editing the number moves the bars live.

        
Blocks: Select mode → drag a rectangle over some gates →
        "Save as block" → pick a letter A–F. From then on, box that letter
        on a wire to place the whole group. Blocks can contain blocks (but
        not themselves).

        
AI sketch: sends a PICTURE of your drawing to an external
        AI service (Z.ai). Its answer is only a proposal — the validator
        checks it, and nothing happens until you press Accept. Without an
        API key you get a canned FAKE example instead.

        
Keep your work: everything autosaves in this browser
        (a refresh brings it back). Save/Load write a JSON file; Qiskit
        exports runnable Python; Data exports your taught letters.

        
Undo / Redo: the buttons, or Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y. A placed
        gate first turns back into your ink, then vanishes stroke by
        stroke.
